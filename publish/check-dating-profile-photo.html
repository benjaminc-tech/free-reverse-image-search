Is this dating profile real?

Search the profile photo on several reverse image engines. If the same face shows up under a different name, on a stock photo site, or on an influencer's account, the profile is very likely using someone else's pictures.

Steps

  1. Save or screenshot the profile photo. Crop it to just the photo so app buttons and text do not confuse the search.
  2. Open the free tool and drop the image in, or tap to pick it from your photos.
  3. Run it through Yandex first, which is usually strongest on faces, then Google Lens, Bing and TinEye.
  4. Look for the same face under another name, on a modeling or stock photo page, or on a public social account with far more history.
  5. Repeat with two or three other photos from the same profile. One clean result proves little; several photos all tracing back to one stranger proves a lot.

What it can't tell you

  • No match does not mean the profile is real. Many genuine people have no public photos, and engines miss plenty.
  • Screenshots carry no camera data, so the metadata panel will usually be empty for these. That is normal.
  • It cannot tell you who is behind the account, only where else the pictures appear.

Questions

Will the person know I searched their photo?

No. Reverse image engines do not notify anyone, and this tool does not upload your image anywhere. Your file stays in your browser until you choose to hand it to an engine.

Which engine is best for faces?

Yandex is usually the strongest for finding the same face elsewhere. Google Lens is better for places and objects. Try both, since each indexes different parts of the web.

The search found nothing. Is the profile safe?

Not necessarily. No match only means none of the engines found a copy. Keep the usual precautions: video call before meeting, and never send money to someone you have not met.
