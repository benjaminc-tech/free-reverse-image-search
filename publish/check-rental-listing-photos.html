Are these rental or marketplace photos stolen?

Reverse search the listing photos. Scam listings often reuse pictures taken from a real listing, a past sale or a hotel site, so a match under a different address, price or seller is a strong warning sign.

Free, no account. Searches Google Lens, Yandex, Bing and TinEye. Nothing is uploaded.

Steps

  1. Save the main listing photos, or copy their image address if the site lets you.
  2. Open the free tool. Drop in a saved photo, or paste an image address to send it to all four engines at once.
  3. Start with Google Lens, which is good at products, places and shopping pages, then Yandex, then TinEye for exact copies.
  4. Compare what you find: a different city, a different price, an older listing, or a rental agency that never heard of this seller.
  5. Check the interior shots too, not just the front of the house. Those are copied just as often and are easier to match.

What it can't tell you

  • A match does not prove fraud on its own. Agents and owners do relist the same photos legitimately, so check the details that differ.
  • No match does not prove the listing is real. Fresh photos of a place the scammer does not own will not show up anywhere.
  • Listing sites usually strip camera data, so the metadata panel will often be empty.

Questions

What are the usual signs of a rental scam besides copied photos?

A price well under the area's going rate, a landlord who cannot show the place in person, and pressure to pay a deposit before you have seen it or signed anything.

Can I search a photo straight from the listing page?

Yes, if you can copy the image address. Paste it into the tool and it sends that address to Google Lens, Yandex, Bing and TinEye in one go.

Does this work for marketplace items, not just rentals?

Yes. A product photo that also appears on a retailer's site or an older listing is worth asking the seller about before you pay.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

Not to this site. A file you load stays in your browser. Only the engine you choose receives the image, when you hand it over.
